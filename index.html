Our process

Every job starts with a written plan

You see exactly what we will cut, how we will do it, and what it costs before anyone starts a saw. The price on the plan is the price on the invoice.

  1. Site walk

    An ISA Certified Arborist inspects each tree on the job, free, usually within three business days.

  2. Written plan and fixed price

    Scope, method (climb, bucket or crane), clean-up and one price. It does not change on the day.

  3. The work

    A crew of three to five sets ground mats, rigs every heavy limb, and follows ANSI Z133 safety practice.

  4. Clean-up and walk-through

    Chips raked, debris hauled, firewood split if you want it. We walk the site with you before we leave.

FAQ

Questions we hear every week

Something else on your mind? Ask during the site walk, or call the office on weekdays from 7 am to 6 pm.

Do I need a permit to remove a tree?

Often, yes. Many towns require one for trees above a set trunk diameter, commonly 6 to 12 inches at chest height, and for any tree near the street. We check your local rules and file the permit for you.

Are you insured?

Yes. We carry $5M in general liability and workers’ compensation for every crew member. We send certificates before we start, to you, your HOA or your property manager.

What happens to the wood?

You choose. We can haul everything away, leave split firewood stacked where you want it, or leave a pile of chips for mulch. Tell us at the site walk and it goes on the plan.

How deep do you grind stumps?

8 to 12 inches below grade as standard, deeper if you plan to plant or pave there. We backfill the hole with the grindings or with topsoil.

When is the best time to prune?

For most trees, late winter while they are dormant. Oaks in oak wilt areas should not be pruned from February through June. Dead or hazardous limbs can come off at any time of year.

Will your equipment damage my lawn?

We lay ground protection mats under every machine and rake the lawn before we leave. If we leave a mark, we repair it.
